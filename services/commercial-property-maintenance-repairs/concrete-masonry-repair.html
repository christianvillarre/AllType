Concrete Base Repair

Repair or replacement planning for damaged concrete bases supporting fencing posts, bollards, signs, and other site fixtures. Base condition, anchors, adjoining concrete, and fixture support are assessed together.

Scope details

Concrete bases supporting signs, bollards, and other site fixtures may be damaged by impact, cracking, or deterioration. We assess the base, exposed anchors, and adjoining concrete before proposing repair or replacement.

Changes to support or anchorage are evaluated as part of the specific fixture scope. For the fixture itself, see handrails and private-property site fixtures.

Parking-Lot Concrete Repair

Localized concrete patching or replacement in commercial parking and service areas. The repair plan considers removal limits, base condition, drainage, loading, and access while the work is completed.

Scope details

We assess localized concrete damage in parking lots, walkways, service areas, and small slabs. The proposed scope distinguishes surface repairs from removal and replacement where the existing concrete cannot support a durable patch.

Repair boundaries, base condition, drainage, loading, and access are reviewed together. Related paving or drainage changes are identified separately so the repair scope is clear.

Stone Repair

Repair assessment for damaged stone finishes and adjoining mortar joints at commercial properties. We distinguish decorative finish repairs from load-bearing masonry and discuss material matching and support needs.

Cinder Block / Concrete Block Installation

Installation and repair planning for concrete masonry units, often called cinder blocks. The scope distinguishes non-load-bearing work from structural walls and establishes materials, reinforcement, support, and any required evaluation.

Concrete Curb Replacement

Removal and replacement planning for damaged commercial curbs at parking edges, islands, and delivery routes. Adjacent pavement, drainage, access, and return-to-service requirements are reviewed before scheduling.

Scope details

Damaged commercial curbs can affect parking edges, landscape islands, and delivery routes. We review the affected curb sections, adjoining pavement, and drainage before defining removal and replacement limits.

Work planning includes pedestrian and vehicle access, protection of adjacent finishes, and the agreed return-to-service requirements.

Commercial Concrete Work

Concrete scopes for small slabs, walkways, service areas, and commercial site improvements. We review loading, base condition, drainage, access, and the intended use before defining materials and work limits.

Stone Repair & Concrete Block Installation

Masonry scopes include damaged stone finishes, mortar joints, and concrete block installation or repairs. Concrete masonry units are often called cinder blocks; the proposed work identifies the actual unit and wall assembly.

Decorative stone and non-load-bearing finish repairs are distinguished from structural masonry work. Wall support, reinforcement, openings, and any required design evaluation are established before work on a structural assembly.

Planning Your Commercial Concrete & Masonry Repair Service

Properties & Operating Hours

We work with Houston shopping centers, storefronts, offices, warehouses, and other commercial buildings. Share tenant hours, delivery schedules, and areas that must remain accessible so the proposed work can be coordinated around operations.

What Affects the Price?

Pricing depends on demolition limits, base condition, concrete depth, reinforcement, material matching, and site access. Assessment establishes the included work, exclusions, and any further evaluation needed before a project price is agreed.

What to Send Us

Provide the property address, onsite contact, affected locations, photos, relevant drawings or past repair information, and desired timing. Identify active damage, tenant concerns, and roof or equipment access restrictions.

Assessment Through Completion

We review the conditions, define the proposed scope, and agree on scheduling and access. At completion, review the agreed work and any remaining items with the project contact. Confirm documentation and follow-up requirements in the proposal.
